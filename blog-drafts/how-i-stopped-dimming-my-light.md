# How I Stopped Dimming My Light (And You Can Too)

**Meta description:** The founder story behind She's The Vibe — how I learned to stop shrinking myself to keep other people comfortable, and how you can stop dimming your light too.

**Target keywords:** stop dimming your light, people pleasing recovery, being yourself unapologetically, self-worth for women, founder story

---

I learned to dim my light before I even knew I had one.

As a kid, I figured out pretty quickly that when I was too much — too curious, too creative, too interested in too many different things — it made my mother uncomfortable. And when she was uncomfortable, I paid for it. So I learned to make myself smaller. Quieter. Easier.

I didn't have the words for it then. I just knew that exploring, trying new things, being more than one box — that got me in trouble. So I stopped. Or I tried to.

Here's the thing about dimming yourself, though: it works. It keeps the peace. People stop being mad at you. The house gets quieter. And for a kid, quiet feels like safety.

But it costs you everything.

## The Slow Disappearing

Nobody sits you down and says "hey, stop being yourself." It's slower than that. It's a look when you get excited about something. It's a sigh when you ask too many questions. It's the message, delivered a thousand tiny times, that who you are naturally is *too much* and you need to tone it down.

So you do. You tone it down. You pick one lane instead of five. You stop raising your hand. You laugh a little quieter. You become very, very good at reading a room and becoming whatever the room needs you to be.

And people love that version of you. She's easy. She's low-maintenance. She doesn't cause problems.

But she's not *you*. She's a performance. And performing is exhausting in a way that sleep doesn't fix.

## The Moment It Cracked

I don't have one dramatic movie moment where I stood up and declared "I'M DONE DIMMING!" Real life doesn't usually work like that.

What I had was a slow accumulation of moments where I caught myself shrinking and thought: *why am I doing this?*

Why am I apologizing for having range? Why am I acting like it's a character flaw that I'm interested in a lot of things? Why am I treating my own personality like it's a problem to be managed?

I'm not one vibe. I've never been one vibe. I'm a planner and a dreamer. I'm soft and I'm disciplined. I'm creative and I'm practical. I'm a mom and a businesswoman and a writer and about six other things depending on the day.

And for years, I treated that like confusion. Like I needed to pick a lane and stay in it to be taken seriously.

Then one day it hit me: **that's not confusion. That's the whole point.**

## What "Stop Dimming" Actually Looks Like

Let me be clear about something, because the internet has turned "stop dimming your light" into an aesthetic. It's not about being loud all the time. It's not about posting inspirational quotes and calling it healing.

Here's what it actually looked like for me:

**I stopped asking for permission to be interested in things.** I didn't need anyone's approval to start a business, write a book, learn something new, or change my mind. I just... did it.

**I stopped shrinking my accomplishments.** When someone asked what I do, I stopped minimizing. I didn't say "oh, just a little Etsy thing." I said I run a lifestyle brand. Because I do.

**I stopped treating my range as a flaw.** I'm not "scattered." I'm not "all over the place." I contain multitudes, and that's a feature, not a bug.

**I built the thing I needed.** She's The Vibe exists because I needed a space like this growing up — a place where a woman can be vibrantly, fully herself without dimming for anybody. Where "too much" isn't a thing. Where you can be every vibe you are, all at once.

## If You're Still Dimming

Maybe you're reading this and recognizing yourself. Maybe you've been making yourself smaller for so long you don't even notice you're doing it anymore.

Here's what I want you to know:

**You don't have to blow up your life to stop dimming.** You don't have to quit your job, cut off your family, or move to a new city. (Unless you want to. No judgment.)

Start small. Notice when you're shrinking. That moment when you're about to say "sorry" for having an opinion? Don't. That moment when you're about to downplay something you're proud of? Don't. That moment when someone's discomfort makes you want to fold yourself in half? Stay standing.

It's going to feel weird at first. When you've spent years being small, taking up space feels like you're doing something wrong. You're not. You're just not used to it yet.

**Find your people.** Not everyone is going to celebrate the full-volume version of you. That's okay. But some people will. Find them. Keep them close. Let them remind you who you are on the days you forget.

**Give yourself the permission you keep waiting for.** Nobody is coming to tap you on the shoulder and say "it's okay to be yourself now." You have to say it. To yourself. Out loud if you need to. *It's okay to be myself now.*

## Why I Built This Brand

She's The Vibe isn't just a shop. It's not just planners and journals and jewelry — though I love all of those things and I'm proud of every product.

It's a declaration. It's me saying: I stopped dimming, and I'm building a whole world around that decision. A world where practical and pretty aren't opposites. Where you can be organized *and* creative, soft *and* strong, a mom *and* a whole person with dreams.

Our motto is "Practical. Pretty. You." And I mean every word of it.

Practical — because real life doesn't pause for aesthetics. You need tools that actually work.

Pretty — because you deserve beautiful things. Not as a reward for being good. Just because.

You — because at the end of the day, that's who this is all for. Not a version of you. Not a future you. You, right now, exactly as you are.

## The Real Talk

If you take one thing from my story, let it be this: the people who need you to dim were never going to let you shine anyway. So you might as well be bright.

You're not too much. You never were. You were just in rooms that were too small for all of you.

Find bigger rooms. Or better yet — build your own.

I did. And you're standing in it.
